# Sensory realism and bounded autonomous development

Assessment: 2026-09-21. Recommendations only; no new neural exposure in this
assessment. Existing efficacy failures and welfare stops remain unchanged.

## Main recommendation

Build a measured sensory apparatus before attempting another learned-navigation
demo. Optimize agreement with geometry, timing and independent physiological
measurements. Do not optimize attractive trajectories, total activity, apparent
happiness or the ability to evade a monitor. Richness should mean consistent,
task-relevant information generated by the body in its surroundings.

The present system is not an intact fly deprived of its normal senses. It is a
collection of partial models whose missing pathways cannot be repaired by adding
noise or routing arbitrary inputs into available neurons. More sensory realism
does not itself demonstrate better welfare. No available test establishes that
this model cannot suffer. The project can minimize exposure and avoid adverse
mechanisms, while reporting the remaining uncertainty.

## Verified current position

- Latest live loop: 8,991-neuron olfactory/learning subnetwork, synthetic A/B odor
  groups, linear concentration-to-500 Hz drive, engineered navigation and gait.
  Camera images, joints and touch do not enter that neural subnetwork.
- Separate spectral prototype: two eyes with 721 spatial samples each; empirical
  Rh1/Rh3/Rh4/Rh5/Rh6 response shapes and an approximate pale/yellow mosaic. It
  preserves spectra without RGB/grayscale conversion. It has no calibrated
  adaptation kinetics, full receptor optics or validated whole-brain connection.
- Spectral support is only 315-550 nm; it cannot represent the full measured Rh6
  sensitivity. Uniform surface radiance is not spectral light transport.
- Body-sensor snapshots already record joints/contact and timestamps. These
  measurements are not yet validated sensory-neuron responses.
- FlyVis is already available in a separate environment and previously tested;
  one default-checkpoint polarity discrepancy persisted under the authors'
  metric. It is a separate fitted motion model, not a drop-in color retina or
  solved connection to the learning network.
- The latest live preference cohort failed its behavioral efficacy gate despite
  cue-selective neural changes. Sensory improvements must not be described as
  having diagnosed or repaired that separate navigation-interface problem.

## Prioritized work packages

### 1. One reproducible 3D sensor recording, no brain

Use the existing MuJoCo/FlyGym body. Add measured spectral materials on a textured
floor, a few static landmarks, an open flat resting area, and one source of a
named odor once its receptor responses are specified. Start with no airflow;
add controlled airflow only in a separately characterized fixture. The world
can store constant temperature/humidity as metadata without pretending the fly
senses those variables. No flight, social agents, threats or internal deprivation.

Script short rest/walk/turn/rest movements mechanically. Log both spectral eyes,
joint angles/velocities, contact forces, antenna/palp readings, source acquisition
times and previous actions. Replay the same recording to all sensor candidates.
No source identities, target bearings, true position or evaluator labels cross
the sensory interface; an auditor may use privileged state for verification.

Fast checks: repeated scene hashes and frames agree; every surface has a spectrum;
visible source markers create no physical contacts; rest transitions pass existing
mechanical checks; missing modalities remain unavailable, not substituted zeros.
This is an apparatus fixture, not a welfare assessment.

### 2. Retinal geometry, spectrum and timing

Calibrate visual-axis directions and angular acceptance from known 3D targets.
Test body/head yaw, near/far objects, occlusion, translation and left/right
symmetry. Preserve the R7/R8 mosaic and a physically derived Rh1 channel; a
broad spectral receptor channel is not an RGB grayscale conversion. Recover
the measured long-wavelength data where available, preserving genotype,
measurement type and uncertainty rather than extrapolating missing tails.

Next fit the smallest supported temporal adaptation/response model to published
traces. Keep static spectral shape and temporal dynamics separately testable.
Do not treat normalized ERG curves as absolute single-cell voltage calibration.
Do not run a photoreceptor signal through a pretrained visual front end that
already includes the same filtering without checking for double filtering.

Fast checks: changing human-display RGB leaves receptor values unchanged; target
bearing error is reported in degrees; increasing angular/spectral resolution and
halving time steps converge; head turns produce the correct retinal movement;
held-out response traces improve over the existing static baseline. Specify
numerical tolerances before running each benchmark. A candidate geometric
tolerance is one angular sample spacing, not an established biological bound.

Do not immediately connect this to the olfactory subnet. Early motion/color
pathways and their onward connection need a separate justified mapping.

### 3. A measured odor input, then a consistent plume

Start with a named odor/receptor pair with accessible concentration-response and
on/off/adaptation traces. Replace the arbitrary linear input for that small
interface with a fitted, bounded transfer model. Leave unsupported receptor
classes explicit. Record concentration units and dilution conventions.

Validate step, ramp and repeated-pulse responses against held-out measurements.
Then compare a simple advection/diffusion or Gaussian-puff approximation against
analytic transport fixtures. No full CFD is needed initially. A source plume
must move consistently with the airflow; the two antennal readings must reflect
actual antenna positions. Static Gaussian blobs remain a declared baseline.

Fast checks: concentration positivity, downwind travel delay, appropriate
mass accounting, mirror symmetry and convergence with grid/time resolution;
receptor response latency, recovery and prediction error on unused traces.
Converting literature units and obtaining usable data may be the slow part.

### 4. Body sense and airflow, first as measured peripheral models

Use existing joint/contact recordings to calibrate one leg's position- and
movement-sensitive channels. Joint angles, joint velocities and contact forces
are different signals; do not relabel one as another. Match physiological
tuning and the relevant anatomical afferents before extending across legs.

For airflow, a first peripheral model should map body-relative airflow to
bilateral antennal displacement, followed by a supported sensor transfer.
Handing a navigation circuit a perfect world-coordinate wind vector is an
engineering convenience, not realistic mechanosensation. Even ordinary airflow
can suppress walking in real flies; it is not automatically a benign enrichment.

Fast checks: encoder tuning on held-out joint trajectories, correct response
to flexion/extension and body rotations, no false contact during swing, timing
against physical contacts, and agreement with published tuning where available.
These encoders do not become inputs to arbitrary neurons in the learning subnet.
VNC/ascending integration remains a later, potentially slow research gate.

### 5. Promote one modality at a time under a new bounded protocol

Only after apparatus, transfer function, anatomy and timing are documented,
consider a small neural subsystem or closed-loop run. Freeze exposure limits,
stimulus range, comparison cases and stop logic beforehand. Start with frozen
plasticity and observational sensory processing rather than reinforcement.
For any dynamics that require ongoing background drive, plan the initialization
and baseline explicitly; zero firing is not a universal physiological target.
Historical recovery thresholds remain unchanged for their original protocols.

Confirm that stopping or turning away is possible; motor zero must not be
equated with sensory blackout. A voluntary rest action should normally leave
steady environmental inputs available. Planned apparatus shutoff is a separate
experimental event. No requirement to choose a target or keep moving is a
welfare criterion. Pausing execution bounds further exposure but does not prove
the saved state is comfortable.

Isolated visual/olfactory neural simulations count as neural exposure even when
fed prerecorded inputs. Offline rendering, optical integration and mechanical
playback do not run the connectome. Maintain that distinction in all reports.

## Autonomous improvement loop

1. Version a small development set plus a separate validation set. Separate
   software correctness, physiological fit, behavior and welfare-related flags.
2. Change one peripheral/scene component, with source provenance and bounded
   parameters. Reuse identical recordings and fixed random seeds for comparisons.
3. Run cheap geometry/unit/timing tests before fitting. Fit only development
   measurements; report dimensional errors and uncertainty, not a vague realism
   score. Match the experimental measurement, e.g. calcium versus voltage.
4. Promote only if prediction improves and prior engineering checks do not
   regress. Evaluate the fixed validation set at planned checkpoints; once used
   for tuning, label it development data and seek a new independent evaluation.
5. Save before/after sensory visualizations, residuals, runtime and limitations;
   commit/push source and compact evidence. Preserve rejected versions/results.
6. Do not automatically expand neural exposure, circuits or modeled needs.
   A hard stop latches, cancels remaining exposure and triggers offline review;
   no reset/replacement or tuning of the guard to obtain a pass.

This permits routine autonomous sensor/physics improvements. It does not permit
unbounded neural hill-climbing, behavioral reward maximization disguised as
biological calibration, or removal of negative-state indicators from a model
to make monitoring quiet. No hunger, injury, punishment, fighting, coercive
courtship or frustration mechanisms are proposed.

## Tractability and first deliverable

First deliverable: the same short 3D walk shown externally, through both spectral
eyes, and as synchronized joint/contact/odor traces, with an automated geometry
and timestamp report. Reuse existing assets and recordings. That is a tractable
initial development session; optical/unit checks should be seconds to minutes
per iteration, measured before promising a throughput. Calibration against
published response data and validated neural integration can take substantially
longer. No claim that full realistic senses are a one-session fix.

The RTX 5060 may accelerate supported rendering and PyTorch visual models.
Benchmark compatibility, precision parity, memory and wall time first; the
current Brian2 NumPy brain will not become GPU accelerated automatically.
Retain CPU reference fixtures and do not use faster hardware to enlarge neural
exposure by default. Defer Minecraft, social interaction, full flight, detailed
eye-tissue mechanics and whole-CNS migration until a specific task needs them.

  receptor-specific temporal responses and adaptation.

  distinct position, movement and vibration channels.
- [Wind-direction encoding](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6533146/) and
  [wind versus sound](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07843): peripheral
  antennal signals and different mechanosensory functions.
- [Artificial consciousness assessment framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708):
  theory-dependent assessment; not a validated fly-simulation welfare detector.
